Confidence intervals for the mean
  • Interval estimate = point estimate ± margin of error
  • Margin of error grows with confidence level and σ, shrinks with √n
  • σ known: x̄ ± z · σ / √n
  • The 95% refers to the procedure, not to one computed interval

Ask A

Thread on slide 14 · 2 questions
Why is the 95% about the procedure? Once I have my interval, μ is either in it or not, right?
Right, and that is the point. μ is fixed, so one computed interval contains it or it doesn't. The 95% describes the method: across many samples, 95% of intervals built this way catch μ. Slide 15 draws exactly that, 20 intervals with one miss. Slide 14Slide 15
So I can't say there's a 95% chance μ is in [4.1, 5.3]?
Correct. Say "95% confident", meaning the procedure works 95% of the time.

Is the t-distribution only for n below 30?
No. Use t whenever σ is unknown. For large n it is almost identical to the normal, so the 30 rule is about the approximation, not the method.
